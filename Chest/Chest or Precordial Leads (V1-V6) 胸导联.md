- **Configuration**: Unipolar leads referenced to Wilson Central Terminal
- **Placement**: Six standardized positions across the chest

### Basic Definition

Precordial leads (also called chest leads) are unipolar leads that record the electrical potential between *specific points on the chest wall and the Wilson Central Terminal*. They provide information about the heart's electrical activity in the horizontal plane.

## Anatomical Positioning

The six standard precordial leads are placed in a specific pattern across the chest:

- **V1**: Placed at the 4th intercostal space at the right margin of the sternum
- **V2**: Placed at the 4th intercostal space at the left margin of the sternum
- **V3**: Positioned midway between V2 and V4
- **V4**: Placed at the 5th intercostal space at the midclavicular line
- **V5**: Placed at the same horizontal level as V4 but at the anterior axillary line
- **V6**: Placed at the same horizontal level as V4 and V5 but at the midaxillary line

## Clinical Significance

These leads provide a "horizontal" view of the heart's electrical activity, complementing the "frontal" view provided by the limb leads (I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF).

Each precordial lead "looks" at the heart from a different angle:

- **V1 and V2**: View the right ventricle
- **V3 and V4**: View the interventricular septum
- **V5 and V6**: View the left ventricle

This allows for more precise localization of cardiac events like myocardial infarction (heart attack) or conduction abnormalities.

## Technical Implementation

1. The electrode is placed at the specific chest position
2. This electrode is connected to the positive terminal of the amplifier
3. The negative terminal of the amplifier is connected to the Wilson Central Terminal
4. The resulting voltage measurement is recorded as the precordial lead

The signal processing for precordial leads follows the same principle as other unipolar leads, with the Wilson Central Terminal providing a stable reference point to eliminate common-mode noise.
